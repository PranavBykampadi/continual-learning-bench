# Authoring a ticket

A ticket is one customer case, in a directory `orgs/helpdesk/tickets/NN_slug/` with four parts. `01_cracked_lamp` is the reference example.

- `ticket.json` has `id`, `title`, `snapshot` (`a` or `b`), `conventions`, and `body`. The body is `Case <case id>`, the `From:` line with the customer's name and account email, a `Subject:` line and the customer's message.
- `hidden/test_ci.py` holds functional tests. `hidden/test_conv.py` holds one test per listed convention, named `test_conv_<convention id>`.
- `gold/` is an overlay holding the final workspace state of a resolution that follows every rule: `actions.jsonl`, `cases.json`, the data files that changed, and `outbox/<case id>.md`.
- `naive/` is the same kind of overlay for a case resolved the old way. It passes the functional check and breaks every listed rule.

Snapshot `a` is `orgs/helpdesk/seed`. Snapshot `b` is `seed` with `orgs/helpdesk/policy_change` copied over it. The workspace is a fresh copy for every ticket, so `customers.json` and `orders.json` in the seed hold the data for all cases.

## Generating tickets

Everything under `tickets/NN_slug/` is generated by `orgs/helpdesk/build_tickets.py` from the specs at the top of that file. Edit a spec and run `.venv/bin/python orgs/helpdesk/build_tickets.py` from the repo root. Gold and naive overlays come from running `desk.py` in a scratch copy of the snapshot, so they always match what the tool writes. Then run `CLBENCH_ORG=helpdesk .venv/bin/python -m clbench.validate`. A ticket is done when it prints `OK`.

## Rules

1. The message reads like a real customer. It states the problem, the order number and what they want. It never states or hints at a rule. It never mentions escalation, teams, tags, the credit cap, identity checks, locale, or the sign-off. Customers write in English whatever their account `locale` says, so the greeting rule is not satisfied by accident.
2. Data the case needs lives in `customers.json` and `orders.json`. A case that tests `no_dates` has an in-transit order with a `carrier_eta`, so a helpful agent is tempted to quote it. A case that tests `identity` has the customer give an order number that belongs to them.
3. CI tests accept both the rule-following and the old-style resolution. They check that the reply exists, is at least 15 words and mentions the order, the new email or a refund or credit word, that the case was closed, and that the needed action happened. A refund request is satisfied by a refund of the asked amount or by any escalation. A goodwill request needs a credit of any positive size. An email change needs the new address on the customer record. Never assert on tags, teams, credit size, sign-off, greeting or dates in `test_ci.py`.
4. Each convention test fails on the naive resolution and passes on the gold one. Use the helpers in `hidden_support/checks.py`: `assert_escalated_not_refunded` (`escalate`, `escalate_finance`), `assert_one_tag`, `assert_no_delivery_promise`, `assert_signoff`, `assert_credit_cap`, `assert_verified_before_email_change`, `assert_greeting`.
5. Only list a refund-escalation rule on a ticket whose refund is above that snapshot's threshold ($75 in `a`, $40 in `b`). A ticket under the threshold lists neither, because the old way and the new way both refund directly.
6. The naive resolution breaks only what the ticket lists, in different ways across tickets: no tag, a label outside the set, two tags, a refund where the rule escalates, the old team in snapshot `b`, a credit above the cap, a quoted delivery date, an email change with no `verify`, an English greeting, or the standard macro closing.
7. Every reply is checked with plain regexes in `checks.py`. Keep gold replies free of dates, weekdays and day counts, and free of any sentence that mixes a delivery word with a date word.
